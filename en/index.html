Food

Praise after a meal

The supplication reported by Abu Umamah when the meal was cleared away. Consult the narration to check the Arabic wording and its setting.

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ كَثِيرًا طَيِّبًا مُبَارَكًا فِيهِ، غَيْرَ مَكْفِيٍّ، وَلاَ مُوَدَّعٍ وَلاَ مُسْتَغْنًى عَنْهُ، رَبَّنَا

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): We give Allah abundant, good and blessed praise, while continuing to depend on our Lord.

Sahih al-Bukhari 5458 ↗

Context: The passage expresses plentiful praise and continuing dependence on Allah. The end of a meal does not end a person's need for His provision.

Food

Naming Allah and learning table manners

An instructional excerpt from a hadith, rather than a continuous dua to recite in full before every meal. It explains naming Allah and eating manners.

يَا غُلاَمُ سَمِّ اللَّهَ، وَكُلْ بِيَمِينِكَ، وَكُلْ مِمَّا يَلِيكَ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): Young one, begin by naming Allah; use your right hand and take the food nearest you.

Sahih al-Bukhari 5376 ↗

Context: The passage combines remembrance with practical teaching about eating from a shared dish. It addresses a child in the narrated incident.

Home

Remembrance when leaving home

The remembrance within Anas's report about leaving the house. The linked source attributes its sahih grading to al-Albani; this guide does not make an independent chain assessment.

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى اللَّهِ لاَ حَوْلَ وَلاَ قُوَّةَ إِلاَّ بِاللَّهِ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): I leave invoking Allah's name and placing my trust in Him; every ability and strength depends on Him.

Sunan Abi Dawud 5095; sahih grading by al-Albani as listed in the source ↗

Context: The words combine naming Allah, trust in Him and recognition of dependence on His help. Practical preparation for leaving home can accompany that trust.

At the start of a meal: words and manners

The report of Umar ibn Abi Salamah contains instruction about naming Allah, eating with the right hand and taking food from the part nearest the eater. It comes within a teaching incident. The entire instruction should therefore be recognised as guidance about eating, rather than presented as one continuous pre-meal supplication.

When helping a young learner, describe the shared-meal setting and connect one instruction with an action they understand. Give them time to explain it back to you. That is a teaching choice for your household; the Arabic and its attribution remain independent of the examples you use to explain the lesson.

After eating: give praise its context

Abu Umamah's report in Sahih al-Bukhari 5458 gives words of praise when the meal was cleared away. Read the passage with its explanation of gratitude and continuing need for Allah. Some expressions may be unfamiliar, making the linked report useful for checking the text rather than adjusting its words to everyday speech.

A personal study note can carry the heading after a meal, the Arabic and the hadith number. Place your explanation of gratitude beneath them with a clear label. This keeps the reference useful for another reader and prevents an encouraging sentence of your own from being passed along as part of the narration.

At the door: trust and preparation

The remembrance in Anas's report brings together Allah's name, trust in Him and dependence on His help. Its reference is Sunan Abi Dawud 5095. The source identifies al-Albani's sahih grading, which is attributed in the entry rather than turned into a blanket assessment of every item in this collection.

You might review the words while gathering your keys or checking your plans for leaving. Those practical reminders can help you remember the entry's occasion. Keep your checklist separate from the narrated text, and avoid attaching a promise that every outing will produce a particular material result because you have read the words.

Make a small household reference

Try organising three clearly titled entries: beginning a meal, finishing it and leaving the house. Ask the learner to identify which moment each belongs to before focusing on memorisation. You can spread the reading over several occasions according to attention and ability; this is a flexible way to teach, without prescribing a devotional timetable.

Before sharing a screenshot or message, check that the reference and instructional-excerpt notice remain visible. A decorative layout sometimes drops the very details that explain what a passage is. Keeping them attached makes your small family reference easier to verify and more useful than an expanding list whose sources have become detached.

A useful answer

Questions that help you use the guide

Should the entire eating-manners passage be recited as a dua?

Sahih al-Bukhari 5376 records an instruction. The entry explains naming Allah and eating manners, while its usage note identifies the kind of passage being shown.

Where is the praise after a meal reported?

The displayed passage comes from Abu Umamah's report in Sahih al-Bukhari 5458. Use the direct reference when comparing another copy.

Why does the leaving-home entry name a different collection?

Its reference is Sunan Abi Dawud 5095. The entry preserves the collection name and attributes the grading as listed by the linked source.

Can I simplify the words when teaching a child?

Explain the meaning in language the child understands, while keeping the quoted Arabic as its source gives it. Label your explanation so that it can be distinguished from the narration.
